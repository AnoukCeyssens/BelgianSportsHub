# Data sources per winter sport (French Alps 2030)

Research for [#4 Data sources per winter sport](https://github.com/AnoukCeyssens/BelgianSportsHub/issues/4). Researched 2026-11-13. Every endpoint marked "probed" was requested on that date with a plain `curl` (no login).

## Summary

**The question:** for each sport on the French Alps 2030 programme, what is the primary source for results, calendar, entry lists and world rankings? How can it be accessed? Does it cover youth and junior events? Can it be filtered by BEL? How fresh is it, and what do the terms of use allow?

**The answer:**

- **The international federation (IF) is the primary source for every sport.** There are seven IFs: FIS (all skiing and snowboard, including freeride), IBU, ISU, IBSF, FIL, WCF and IIHF. The eighth source is ISMF for ski mountaineering. Junior and youth events, including YOG and EYOF, sit in the same IF databases. The one exception is freeride, where the federation database is still empty and the tour's own site (Freeride World Tour, owned by FIS) holds the data.
- **One sport has a clean, open, documented API: ISU speed skating** (`api.isuresults.eu`). It has an OpenAPI schema, needs no key to read, and filters by country with `?country=BEL`. Senior and junior World Cups and Championships are included.
- **FIS is the richest source for the most Belgians, but its terms explicitly forbid scraping.** It covers alpine, cross-country, ski jumping, freestyle/freeski and snowboard. The FIS database is server-rendered HTML with a `nationcode=BEL` filter. Better still, FIS publishes **FIS points list ZIPs** with tab-separated CSVs of competitors, races, results and points, refreshed roughly weekly. That is near-ideal for ingest. However, the FIS terms say *"You may not scrape the content on the FIS Digital Products"*, and they forbid reproduction without written permission.
- **IBU closed its de facto API.** The `biathlonresults.com/modules/sportapi` endpoints that community tools used now return **HTTP 401** to anonymous requests (probed). Biathlon would mean a login-gated app or manual curation, unless IBU grants access.
- **Everything else is HTML scraping, sometimes with PDFs:**
  - FIL (luge): server-rendered, nation filter on athletes.
  - WCF (curling): classic server-rendered results database with per-association pages.
  - ISU figure skating and synchro: static per-event HTML from the timing contractor.
  - ISU short track: client-rendered pages backed by an undocumented API.
  - ISMF (skimo, via dataskimo.org): simple PHP pages.
  - Freeride World Tour: Next.js.
  - IBSF and IIHF could not be fetched from this environment (TLS interception and a bot wall), so they are *unverified*.
- **Freshness is not a problem anywhere.** Results go up the same day, which meets the daily need during Championships and Games. World Cup standings update after each event. FIS points lists come out about weekly in season.
- **Terms are the real constraint, as they were for summer sports.** FIS bans scraping outright. The IBU terms forbid reproduction without written permission. ISU, WCF, FIL, ISMF and IBSF publish no data licence that I found. ISU and ISMF/dataskimo carry Cloudflare `Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-train=no` in robots.txt, which says nothing either way about a private app.

**Implication for the spec:**
- Automatic ingest is clearly fine only for **ISU speed skating**.
- It is technically trivial for **FIS**, using the points-list ZIPs plus the BEL-filtered biography pages. It should be done **only after asking FIS for permission**. Otherwise treat FIS as "manual curation assisted by a manually downloaded weekly ZIP".
- For biathlon, sliding, curling, ice hockey, skimo and freeride, Belgian volume is small: a handful of athletes, plus national teams in curling and hockey. **Manual curation from IF pages** fits better than building seven scrapers.

## French Alps 2030 programme

- **Programme is final.** The IOC Executive Board approved the Alpes 2030 event programme on **7 July 2026**. Sources: the [IOC news release](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/alpes-2030-to-be-first-gender-equal-olympic-winter-games-freeride-ski-and-snowboard-and-synchro9-figure-skating-athletes-to-debut) (seen in search results; the page timed out on fetch) and [FIS's reaction, dated 7 July 2026](https://fis-ski.com/inside-fis/news/2025-26/fis-reaction-to-the-announcement-of-the-olympic-winter-games-alpes-2030-program) (fetched).
- **Ski mountaineering was confirmed separately** as an additional sport by the 146th IOC Session on 25 June 2026, at Montgenèvre ([IOC](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/ski-mountaineering-approved-as-additional-sport-for-alpes-2030-olympic-winter-games)).
- **Changes from Milano-Cortina 2026:**
  - **Nordic combined dropped.** It stays on the Dolomiti Valtellina 2028 YOG programme, and FIS keeps its World Cup.
  - **Freeride added:** ski and snowboard, men and women, 4 events and 44 athletes.
  - **Synchro9 synchronised skating added** within figure skating.
  - New events confirmed by FIS: ski jumping women's super team, ski cross mixed team and snowboard mixed team parallel.
  - New events from secondary summaries only: biathlon mixed single relay and speed skating team sprints (men and women).
- **Totals: 126 events and 3,046 athletes** (1,525 women and 1,521 men). These figures come from [Canadian Olympic Committee](https://olympic.ca/?p=354728) and [CONI](https://rivistadirittosportivo.coni.it/en/news/new-disciplines-additional-events-on-the-programme-and-gender-equality-whats-new-at-the-winter-olympic-games.html) summaries of the IOC decision. I did not read the official event-programme PDF, so treat the per-event additions as *secondary*.

Resulting sports and disciplines in scope: **skiing** (alpine, cross-country, ski jumping, freestyle, snowboard, freeride), **biathlon**, **ski mountaineering**, **skating** (speed skating, short track, figure skating including synchro9), **bobsleigh** (bobsleigh, skeleton), **luge**, **curling** and **ice hockey**. Nordic combined is out of scope for 2030. It still matters for YOG 2028, which counts as an International-level activity for juniors.

## Notes by source

- **FIS points-list ZIP**, probed with `ALFP2027F.zip`. The 20th list of 2026/27 was calculated 2026-11-10 and is valid 2026-11-12 to 18. It contains 10 tab-separated files:
  - `com`: Competitorid, Fiscode, names, gender, birthdate, Nationcode, status
  - `rac`: Raceid, Eventid, date, place, category, discipline
  - `res`: Raceid, Competitorid, position, time, racepoints, Nationcode
  - `pts`: points and position per discipline
  - `evt`, `cat`, `dis`, `hdr`, `team`, and `Fiscategory.txt` (category codes incl. EYOF, YOG, WJC, OWG)
  - The **F** variant is the full file and **P** is likely points-only. The race set covers races valid for FIS points in the list window.
  - This is the closest thing to an official bulk feed in winter sport. One weekly file would cover athletes, races and results for five Olympic disciplines. It is a published download, but automated fetching still falls under the T&C's scraping clause. **Ask FIS.**
- **FIS legacy XML.** FIS documents an XML data-exchange format ([XMLdescription PDF, 2007](https://assets.fis-ski.com/f/252177/1bc53d82fa/xmldescription.pdf)). It is meant for organisers and timing providers sending results *to* FIS, not as a public feed.
- **IBU change.** Community tools (realbiathlon, PyPI wrappers) were built on the anonymous Datacenter API. As of 2026-11-13 it answers 401. I did not look for or use a token. Any revival would need IBU's permission.
- **Olympic Games themselves.** During the Games, the IF sites mirror results, but the official Games results come from the OCOG/IOC (Olympics.com). That belongs to the sibling aggregator ticket.

## Could not verify

- The per-event list of the Alpes 2030 programme. I relied on secondary summaries of the IOC decision because olympics.com timed out.
- Whether FWT ranking data exposes nationality, and whether ISMF/dataskimo can filter by nation or junior category.
- Where synchro9 and the short-track results are hosted at machine level (short track is behind an undocumented `front-api.isu.org`).
- `GLOSSARY.md` is not on `origin/main` yet, so terms follow the map issue's wording.

## Open questions (candidates for new tickets)

1. **Ask FIS and IBU for written permission.** A short e-mail describing a private, non-commercial app for a small circle. FIS covers 5 of the Olympic skiing disciplines, which is likely where most Belgian winter athletes are, and the T&C explicitly ban scraping. IBU closed its API. This decides whether FIS can be "auto" or must be "manual".
2. **Freeride qualification path to 2030.** Is the Olympic quota based on FWT, FIS Freeride World Championships or a new FIS freeride ranking? This decides which source is primary for freeride.
3. **Manual curation tooling.** If about 7 of the 13 sources stay manual, what is the input workflow? For example, paste a results URL and pick the BEL rows. This links to the map's "hybrid" data note.
4. **Team sports granularity.** Are curling and ice hockey tracked as national teams only, or by player? IIHF and WCF have player rosters, but the scope rule counts athletes.
