# LA28 and French Alps 2030 Olympic qualification systems

Research for [#6](https://github.com/AnoukCeyssens/BelgianSportsHub/issues/6). Researched 2026-11-13.

## Summary

- **LA 2028 (summer): almost fully published.** The IOC Executive Board (EB) approved the first 26 systems in December 2025 and had approved 49 sports and disciplines by May 2026. Cricket followed on 29 June 2026, and World Athletics published athletics on 10 November 2026. The only unverified piece is football: FIFA set its confederation slots in December 2025, but I couldn't find the IOC-approved document. All systems use one IOC template and are bound by the [LA28 Qualification System Principles](https://olympic.org.na/uploads/documents/LA2028-Qualification-System-Principles-130726-100218.pdf) (QSP; approved March 2025, current version 12 May 2026).
- **French Alps 2030 (winter): not published.** The IOC EB fixed the event programme and athlete quotas on 7 July 2026. That decision dropped Nordic combined and added freeride, ski mountaineering and new mixed or team events. I found no qualification system yet, and no 2030 Qualification System Principles either. Milano-Cortina 2026 is the precedent: principles in September 2022, systems approved March–December 2023, so about 26–35 months before the Games. On that pattern, expect the 2030 systems **from about early 2027 to early 2028**, after a 2030 QSP that is probably adopted around now (late 2026 to 2027).
- **One status model across sports is feasible only if it separates the NOC's place from the athlete.** The template requires every system to declare whether a quota place is **allocated to the NOC or to the athlete by name**, and many sports mix both within one sport, event by event or pathway by pathway. On top of that, Belgium layers its own **BOIC selection** step. So "Belgium has a quota place" and "athlete X is going" are different facts, and in NOC-owned sports there can be weeks between them.
- **BOIC selection** uses a fixed four-layer structure. It was identical for Paris 2024 and Milano-Cortina 2026: (1) Belgium holds a quota place and the BOIC confirms it will use it; (2) the IF's eligibility and minimum-performance criteria; (3) optional, *exceptional* "Special Criteria" that are stricter than the IF's; (4) "Internal Criteria" that decide which eligible Belgian gets an NOC-owned place. The BOIC board selects on the proposal of the national federation. I could not find the LA28 BOIC general regulation; for Paris it was published on 26 July 2022, two years out.

## IOC frame (applies to every LA28 sport)

From the [LA28 QSP](https://olympic.org.na/uploads/documents/LA2028-Qualification-System-Principles-130726-100218.pdf) (a mirror of the IOC document; olympics.com timed out from this environment):

| Rule | LA28 value |
|---|---|
| Who writes the system | Each IF, approved by the IOC EB, in the IOC template; the official copy sits on Olympics.com |
| Qualification period | No longer than 24 months |
| Last qualification event | Sunday **11 June 2028** |
| Sport entries deadline | Monday **26 June 2028** |
| Games | 14–30 July 2028 |
| Opportunities | More than one, "where possible" directly at a continental or world event; ranking-based systems should also offer direct qualification |
| Continental representation | Required, preferably through Continental Multisport Games (Pan Ams, Asian Games, European Games, …) |
| Universality places | Required in individual sports, not in team sports; allocated by the Tripartite Commission (call for requests 1 Oct 2027, deadline 15 Jan 2028 per the [archery system](https://olympic.org.na/uploads/documents/ARC-LA28-Qualification-System-170726-054617.pdf)) |
| Host country places | Required (United States), sometimes subject to a minimum performance |
| Quota ownership | Each system must say whether the place goes to the **NOC** or to the **athlete by name**. Even a named place is entered by the NOC, which "may have stricter eligibility/selection criteria" |
| NOC confirmation | At least 5 working days to accept or decline; the decision is final |
| Reallocation | Named-athlete sports must reallocate up to 48 h before competition; NOC-owned sports use the Late Athlete Replacement policy (published by the 2027 Chefs de Mission seminar) |
| Changes | Only via IOC review; IOC keeps a change log on Olympics.com |

Belgium is neither host nor (realistically) universality-eligible. These places matter to the app only because they take places out of the pool.

## Per-sport table: French Alps 2030 (precedent: Milano-Cortina 2026)

The 2030 systems are not published. The IOC EB approved the programme and quotas on 7 July 2026: FIS disciplines get 1,264 places, freeride is added and Nordic combined is dropped ([FIS](https://www.fis-ski.com/inside-fis/news/2025-26/fis-reaction-to-the-announcement-of-the-olympic-winter-games-alpes-2030-program)); luge gets 106 places in 5 events ([FIL](https://www.fil-luge.org/de/news/alpes-2030-with-106-luge-athletes)); ski mountaineering was confirmed as an additional sport ([IOC](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/ski-mountaineering-approved-as-additional-sport-for-alpes-2030-olympic-winter-games)).

The MiCo 2026 systems were approved in batches during 2023: ice hockey in March; biathlon, curling, figure skating, short track and ski mountaineering in June; speed skating in September; luge, cross-country, freestyle, Nordic combined, ski jumping and snowboard in October; alpine, bobsleigh and skeleton in late November / December ([IOC](https://olympics.com/ioc/news/ioc-approves-six-qualification-systems-and-optimises-if-delegation-sizes-for-milano-cortina-2026); [IOC](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/the-ioc-approves-final-qualification-systems-and-competition-schedule-by-session-for-milano-cortina-2026)).

The mechanisms below describe the MiCo 2026 pattern as I know it from previous cycles. **I did not re-read the MiCo documents in this session**, so treat them as the expected shape, not verified facts.

| Sport | MiCo 2026 pattern | Quota belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| Alpine skiing | FIS points list + World Cup/Worlds results; "basic quota" of 1M + 1W for any NOC meeting minimum points; extra places by ranking | NOC |
| Biathlon | **Nation ranking** (World Cup + IBU Cup Nations Cup score) sets NOC quota size; athletes need IBU qualification criteria | NOC |
| Bobsleigh / skeleton | IBSF ranking → sleds per NOC, continental guarantees | NOC |
| Cross-country / ski jumping / freestyle / snowboard / freeride (new) | FIS Olympic Quota Allocation List from FIS points/World Cup; minimum FIS points or a top-30 result for eligibility | NOC |
| Curling | Points from Worlds + Olympic Qualification Event | NOC |
| Figure skating | Worlds placements + final qualifier; team event from combined standings | NOC |
| Ice hockey | IIHF World Ranking + final qualification tournaments | NOC |
| Luge | FIL World Cup quota points | NOC |
| Short track / speed skating | **Special Olympic Qualification Classification (SOQC)** from World Cup / World Tour, by distance; time standards (speed) | NOC |
| Ski mountaineering | ISMF ranking + events | NOC |
| Nordic combined | Dropped for 2030 | n/a |

Winter sports are therefore close to uniformly **NOC-owned**, and that ownership is what makes the BOIC/federation internal criteria decide which Belgian skates or skis. The Belgian short-track example below shows it.

## Belgian Olympic Committee (BOIC) layer

Primary sources: the Belgian federation documents published under the BOIC regulation, namely [swimming, Paris 2024](https://www.belswim.be/storage/files/file/1040.pdf) (v2.3, 6 Jul 2023) and [short track, MiCo 2026](https://www.kbsf.be/assets/documenten/shorttrack/critsel_STK_MiCo_2026_-_NL_-_Version_1_0.pdf) (v1.0, 24 Jan 2025). Background: [BOIC news, 26 Jul 2022](https://www.teambelgium.be/nl/artikels/boic-beslist-over-selectiecriteria-parijs-2024).

- The BOIC adopts a **general selection regulation** per Games. Each federation then publishes sport-specific criteria on teambelgium.be, and only the BOIC-hosted version is valid.
- An athlete is selected only if they meet all four criteria layers and the **BOIC Board** approves the federation's proposal:
  1. **General:** a quota place is allocated to Belgium and the BOIC confirms it will use it. The BOIC can decline a place.
  2. **International:** the IF's eligibility and minimum-performance criteria.
  3. **Special (optional):** "exceptionally" stricter than the IF's, set by the federation and approved by the BOIC. Both examples chose none. For reallocated places the swimming document drops the Special Criteria altogether.
  4. **Internal (optional):** the ranking among eligible Belgians for a place, e.g. short track's internal start-place ranking based on the SOQC, or swimming's "best time at preferred meets" when more than two swimmers hold an A time.
- For Paris 2024 the BOIC said it would "apply the international selection criteria as the reference for participation". I found no general BOIC-wide "top-8 perspective" rule in the Paris or MiCo material. For an LA28 preparation camp the BOIC did use the chance of an Olympic diploma (top 8) as a criterion ([teambelgium.be](https://www.teambelgium.be/fr/nouvelles/le-team-belgium-a-belek-en-vue-des-jeux-de-los-angeles-2028)), but that is not selection.
- **LA28 / 2030 BOIC regulations: not found** as of 2026-11-13. For Paris it appeared about 24 months before the Games, so LA28's is due now or soon. Expect 2030's around early 2028.

## Cross-sport structural patterns

These are the dimensions a single status model has to cover.

1. **Who owns the quota.** There are three cases:
   - NOC-owned: road, track and MTB cycling, rowing, sailing, hockey and all team sports, equestrian, triathlon, taekwondo, wrestling, archery, and probably every winter sport.
   - Athlete-named: boxing, judo, tennis, golf individual, skateboarding, climbing, squash, swimming individual, open water, modern pentathlon.
   - Mixed within one sport: artistic and rhythmic gymnastics, shooting, surfing, table tennis, golf (individual named, mixed team NOC), swimming (individual named, relays NOC), athletics (marathon and race walk named).

   Even a named place still has to be accepted by the NOC, and the NOC can apply stricter criteria. Judo and modern pentathlon also let the NOC choose when several of its athletes qualify.
2. **Unit of qualification.** The unit can be an athlete, a boat or crew (rowing lets the crew change for places won at Worlds but not for places won at regattas), a pair (beach volleyball, doubles), a team (team sports, team pursuit, equestrian teams of 3), or a **nation ranking** that earns a *count* of places (road cycling 4/3/2/1, biathlon, FIBA 3x3 NOC ranking). In the last case an individual athlete only *contributes* points.
3. **Mechanism.** Most sports combine several:
   - Ranking cut-off on a fixed date: judo, golf, tennis, triathlon, skateboarding, weightlifting, shooting's Olympic Ranking, 3x3.
   - Qualifying events: Worlds, continental championships or Continental Multisport Games, dedicated Olympic qualifiers or Last Chance Regattas.
   - Performance standards: athletics entry standards, swimming A/B times, archery MQS, squash top 50.

   A single athlete can therefore be *provisionally in* on a live ranking long before any place is final.
4. **Hierarchical and conditional allocation.** Pathways are processed "in hierarchical order". Places won twice cascade to the next athlete, host or continental places shift other places, and some places are explicitly provisional (e.g. NOCs at FIH qualifiers depend on continental results). Status can therefore change without the athlete competing.
5. **Continental places.** These exist almost everywhere and favour Europe less than world rankings do (judo: 26 of 104 continental places go to Europe). For Belgians, European Championships and the European Games are therefore real qualification events, e.g. EuroHockey 2027 and the 2027 jumping Europeans in Waregem.
6. **Universality and host places** don't apply to Belgium, but they shrink and reshape the pool.
7. **Reallocation and confirmation.** NOCs have at least 5 working days to accept or decline. Declined or unused places are reallocated until just before the Games (named-athlete sports up to 48 h before competition; NOC-owned sports through Late Athlete Replacement). "Reserve / next in line" is a real state.
8. **Two independent gates for Belgians.** There is the IF/IOC quota gate (an NOC place or a named place) and the BOIC selection gate (the federation proposes, the BOIC board decides), with athlete-level Internal Criteria in between for NOC-owned places.
9. **Timing.** LA28: qualification windows mostly mid-2026 to May/June 2028, events end 11 June 2028, entries due 26 June 2028. 2030: systems are not out yet, and by the MiCo pattern the windows run roughly mid-2028 to January 2030.

A model that fits every sport probably needs a **NOC quota place** entity (sport, event, NOC, pathway, status: provisional/confirmed/declined/reallocated) kept separate from an **athlete's Olympic status** (e.g. in contention → provisionally qualified (named) or contributing to an NOC place → nominated by federation → selected by BOIC → entered). This is a hypothesis for the grilling ticket, not a finding.
